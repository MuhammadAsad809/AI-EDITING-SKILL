# Phonk Editing Grammar

Phonk is an editorial language built from bass weight, rhythmic accents, contrast, attitude, and a strong subject. The visual identity varies by subgenre; do not treat every Phonk track as a fast car montage.

## Subgenre grammar

| Style | Emotional target | Pacing | Useful visual language | Restraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classic Memphis | Haze, menace, nostalgia | Long hypnotic holds with selective accents | Grain, VHS, warm/desaturated color, texture | Avoid modern hyper-cutting unless intentionally hybrid |
| Drift | Danger, momentum, mechanical power | Build from 2–4 beats toward 1-beat impacts | Directional movement, ramps, wheel/detail contrast, bass hits | Preserve car silhouette and driving direction |
| Brazilian | Kinetic crowd energy and syncopation | Dense, but phrase-led rather than random | Quick reframes, saturated color, rhythmic body movement | Protect faces and avoid indiscriminate strobe |
| Gym/hype | Effort, control, transformation | Readable reps and decisive impacts | Wide-to-close progression, breath, controlled shake | Do not obscure technique with overlays |
| Sigma/meme | Character attitude and recognition | Medium-fast punctuation | Freeze frames, punch-ins, captions, graphic motifs | Use humor and text sparingly; keep the face legible |

## The Phonk arc

**Hook:** open with a result, hero pose, car silhouette, or unusual frame. **Setup:** identify the subject and visual motif. **Build:** increase motion, shot-size contrast, or sound density. **Escalation:** make the action or attitude more specific. **Drop:** land the clearest, strongest shot with one dominant impact. **Release:** hold, slow, or react. **End:** close the thought, loop cleanly, or leave a deliberate after-image.

## Layer hierarchy

1. Clean cut and shot order.
2. Structural beat changes and phrase edits.
3. Meaningful accents on kick, bass, cowbell, or vocal.
4. Speed design and motion emphasis.
5. Sound effects and ambience.
6. Grade and texture.
7. Graphic overlays and ornamental effects.

If a lower layer weakens a higher layer, remove the lower layer. A glitch cannot repair a weak drop; a LUT cannot repair mismatched footage; a shake cannot make an unclear action legible.

## Effect decision table

| Need | First choice | Optional support | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Make movement feel heavier | Bass hit, clean impact cut | Small scale pulse or 1–2 frame shake | Permanent shake |
| Connect directional motion | Match-on-action or hard cut | Short directional blur | Spin transition with no direction |
| Build anticipation | Longer hold, reverse, riser, acceleration | Brief dip to black | Constant flashes |
| Mark a memory/retro section | Dissolve, ambience, grain | VHS texture | Heavy degradation over faces |
| Emphasize character/attitude | Hold, crop, caption, reaction | Freeze frame | Covering the face with skulls/text |
