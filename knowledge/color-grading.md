# Color Correction and Grading

Separate correction from look design. First balance exposure, white balance, contrast, and skin tones; then create a look that supports story and genre. Match shots before applying a global style.

Use scopes when available: waveform for exposure, vectorscope for saturation and skin-tone consistency, and RGB parade for color balance. Protect highlights and faces. A LUT is a starting transform, not a finished grade; verify that the source color space and exposure are appropriate.

| Intent | Useful direction | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Clean commercial | Neutral skin, controlled contrast, selective saturation | Plastic skin or over-sharpening |
| Moody/cinematic | Lowered saturation, shaped shadows, motivated warmth | Crushed blacks and lost detail |
| Neon/music video | Controlled hue separation and bright accents | Skin contamination and chroma noise |
| Vintage | Gentle contrast, warm/cool bias, texture | Nostalgia applied to every scene |

Grade in shot groups, then review the sequence continuously. A look must remain consistent enough for the viewer to follow the story.
