# Sound Design

Sound sells the cut. Build tracks in layers: dialogue/VO, production and ambience, music, spot effects, designed transitions, and master control.

## Editorial relationships

- Use J-cuts and L-cuts to make scene changes feel intentional.
- Place a whoosh on meaningful motion, not on every cut.
- Use an impact or bass hit for a visual landing; leave headroom before it.
- Duck music under dialogue and key sound effects; do not simply make dialogue louder.
- Use ambience to connect shots from different locations and preserve spatial continuity.

Clean dialogue before color or loudness polish. High-pass rumble only as needed, reduce harshness conservatively, compress for consistency, and automate levels by phrase. Reverb should describe a space or create a deliberate memory effect; excessive reverb reduces intelligibility.

## Mix check

Listen on headphones, speakers, and at low volume. Check mono compatibility, clipped peaks, abrupt room-tone changes, and whether the intended impact still reads without the picture. Keep final loudness and true-peak targets platform-appropriate and verify current delivery specifications before export.
