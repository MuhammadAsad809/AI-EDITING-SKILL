# Beat-Sync Editing

## Beat math

At BPM `B`, one quarter-note beat lasts `60,000 / B` milliseconds. An eighth note is half that duration; a sixteenth is one quarter. For a 150 BPM track, a beat is 400 ms, an eighth is 200 ms, and a sixteenth is 100 ms. Treat these as a grid, not a prison: phrase changes and visual readability outrank mechanical cutting.

## Musical hierarchy

Mark bar starts and downbeats first. Then mark kicks, snares, bass hits, vocal consonants, risers, drops, and instrumentation changes. Use downbeats for section changes or hero shots, kicks for movement, snares for angle or reaction changes, bass for weight, and vocals for meaning-led cuts.

## Beat map pattern

`bar 3, beat 1 / downbeat → wide establishing shot → camera move begins → low impact`

`bar 3, beat 2 / snare → close-up → match gaze → short whoosh`

`bar 3, beat 3 / kick → action completion → 8% punch-in → bass accent`

`bar 3, beat 4 / vocal hit → reaction or graphic → caption appears → duck music 1–2 dB if dialogue exists`

## Sync modes

- **Structural sync:** cut or change visual language on a phrase, bar, drop, or chorus.
- **Accented sync:** align a meaningful action or camera movement to a kick, snare, bass, or vocal hit.
- **Texture sync:** use rapid cuts, flashes, or typography on subdivisions only during a prepared peak.
- **Anticipation:** accelerate, hold, silence, or reverse before the impact so the landing is perceived.

If the source has swing, syncopation, or a loose live feel, place edits by musical accent rather than forcing a straight grid.
