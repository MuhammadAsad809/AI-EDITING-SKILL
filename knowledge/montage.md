# Montage Construction

Montage is controlled compression: a sequence of shots whose order, duration, repetition, and contrast create a new idea or rising energy. It is not simply “many fast clips.”

## Seven-part arc

1. **Hook:** show the most legible promise or unusual image.
2. **Setup:** establish subject, place, rules, or visual motif.
3. **Build-up:** increase variation, shot size, movement, or musical density.
4. **Escalation:** shorten holds and intensify contrast; remove unnecessary explanation.
5. **Peak:** reserve the strongest action, reveal, frame, or impact.
6. **Release:** allow a longer hold, breath, reaction, or tonal change.
7. **Ending:** resolve, loop, or point toward the next action.

## Build from raw footage

Bin clips by action, size, direction, quality, and emotional value. Select a hero clip for the peak, two or three support motifs, reaction or texture shots, and at least one release image. Build a silent paper edit first. Then map the song’s intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus/drop, bridge, and outro to the arc.

## Energy curve

Represent each section on a 1–5 scale for cut density, motion, sound density, and emotional intensity. Increase at least two dimensions before the peak; do not max all four from the opening frame. Contrast is useful: a 12-frame flash is more powerful after a 2-second hold.

## Montage test

Mute the sequence. If the visual arc is unreadable, the music will not rescue it. Then listen without looking: if every sound is at maximum intensity, the peak has no contrast.
