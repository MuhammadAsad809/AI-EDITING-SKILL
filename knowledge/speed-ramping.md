# Speed Ramping and Time Manipulation

A useful ramp has a reason: reveal a decisive action, bridge two shots, anticipate an impact, or create a breath after intensity.

## Five-phase design

1. **Baseline:** establish natural motion so the viewer understands time.
2. **Anticipation:** accelerate 130–250% or tighten a hold as the action approaches.
3. **Impact:** land the decisive frame at normal speed, a brief 40–60% slow motion, or a freeze frame.
4. **Release:** return toward baseline with a smooth curve rather than an abrupt toggle.
5. **Recovery:** allow the next shot to be readable before another manipulation.

Use optical flow or frame interpolation only when it produces clean motion; inspect edges, hands, hair, and repeated patterns. Use motion blur for fast movement, but do not use blur to conceal bad interpolation. Freeze frames work best when paired with a graphic, sound hit, or clear story punctuation. Reverse motion should motivate a rewind, anticipation, or stylized transition.

Always specify the ramp points, approximate percentages, curve shape, beat alignment, and what happens to sound. Preserve a natural option for dialogue and safety-critical action.
