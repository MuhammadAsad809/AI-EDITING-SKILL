# Transitions

A transition is a relationship between shots, not a decoration. Start with a hard cut. Add another transition only when it communicates time, space, motion, tone, or a deliberate graphic connection.

| Transition | Purpose | Best use | Avoid | Strength / alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dissolve | Passage of time or shared emotion | Memory, geography, gentle chapter change | Routine action cuts | 6–16 frames; use a J-cut if audio can bridge |
| Dip to black/white | Clear section or emotional break | Endings, impact, flash of light | Hiding weak coverage | 2–12 frames; hard cut if momentum matters |
| Whip/motion blur | Connect directional movement | Matched pans or fast action | Unmatched directions | Keep blur brief; use a match-on-action |
| Object wipe/mask | Hide a cut in foreground movement | Passing person, car, wall, darkness | No foreground cover | Track accurately; use a clean cut if edge quality fails |
| Zoom/spin/glitch | Stylized genre signal | Intentional music-video or gaming moment | Every beat, unrelated footage | One clear accent; use scale keyframes or a graphic match |
| Speed transition | Connect clips through velocity | Travel, car, sports, action | Dialogue or calm scenes | Ramp into movement; use a cut if motion is not continuous |

Common failure modes are mismatched direction, inconsistent blur, visible mask edges, excessive duration, and using a transition where a cut would be clearer.
