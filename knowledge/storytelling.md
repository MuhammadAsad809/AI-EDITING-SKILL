# Storytelling and Pacing

An edit creates expectation, information, and consequence. Define the promise, introduce the subject or question, escalate obstacles or sensory intensity, deliver a payoff, and give the viewer a release or next step.

Pacing is not the same as speed. It is the rate at which meaningful information changes. A longer shot can be fast when it contains suspense; a rapid montage can feel slow when every frame says the same thing. Use pauses before reveals, reaction shots after events, and contrast between sections.

For long-form content, outline chapters, each with a local hook, development, turn, and exit. Use B-roll to clarify or compress ideas, not to hide every edit. Preserve silence when it carries thought, tension, or authenticity.
