# Motion Design

Motion should direct attention and express energy. Use keyframes for position, scale, rotation, opacity, and anchor point; use eased curves rather than linear movement unless mechanical precision is intended.

Subtle push-ins (roughly 2–8% over a shot) can add life to a static image. A parallax move is credible only when foreground and background separation is clean. Camera shake should be short, directional, and reserved for impact; stabilize first when the shot is meant to feel controlled. Track text or masks to the subject, then check for drift at the first, middle, and last frame.

For kinetic typography, animate the word that carries meaning, not every word equally. Match entry and exit to phrasing; preserve contrast and safe margins. Motion blur, overshoot, and bounce are stylistic choices—set them intentionally and reduce them when readability suffers.
