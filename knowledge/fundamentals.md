# Editing Fundamentals

## Editorial hierarchy

A professional edit answers three questions in order: **What is the viewer looking at? Why should they care now? What should they feel or understand next?** Cutting, sound, color, and graphics are instruments for those answers. A technically flashy sequence with no changing intention is not a strong edit.

## Core operations

- **Trim:** shorten a clip at its head or tail while preserving surrounding timing.
- **Split:** create an edit point without changing duration.
- **Ripple edit:** remove or insert material while closing or opening the gap; verify sync on downstream tracks.
- **J-cut:** incoming audio begins before its picture, preparing the next idea.
- **L-cut:** outgoing audio continues under the next picture, preserving thought or emotional residue.
- **Cutaway/B-roll:** cover a continuity problem or add evidence, texture, or a useful change of visual scale.

Use continuity editing when spatial and temporal clarity are the priority. Break continuity deliberately when disorientation, shock, memory, or compression is the point. Preserve screen direction and eyelines unless the break is motivated by a clear story beat.

## A reliable assembly order

1. Sync and label media.
2. Select story-critical dialogue or action.
3. Build a radio edit or silent action spine.
4. Add coverage and B-roll.
5. Shape pacing and remove dead air.
6. Add music, effects, color, titles, and polish.
7. Review at normal speed, muted, and audio-only.

## Quality control

Ask whether every shot earns its place, whether the viewer can identify the subject, whether each cut changes information or energy, whether audio leads or trails intentionally, and whether the final moment provides closure or a loop. Never solve an unclear story by adding a transition.
