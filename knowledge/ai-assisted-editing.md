# AI-Assisted Phonk Editing

AI should accelerate repetitive work and generate supporting material, not replace editorial timing. Use the simplest tool that solves the task.

## Appropriate uses

- Transcribe dialogue or vocal samples and create captions.
- Detect beat candidates, then verify the grid manually against the actual track.
- Generate a rough clip inventory from filenames, thumbnails, or metadata.
- Remove backgrounds, extend a plate, create a texture, or generate a short insert when real footage cannot provide the required image.
- Upscale or denoise a genuinely valuable shot while checking for halos, waxy skin, and repeated textures.
- Produce alternate title treatments, overlays, or concept frames for review.

## Inappropriate shortcuts

Do not let an automatic beat-sync tool determine the whole edit, use a generated car or person when the real subject carries authenticity, or add a template whose speed, flashes, and captions cannot be controlled. Treat generated content as a shot with continuity, legal, safety, and artifact risks.

## Prompting generated clips

Specify subject, environment, lens/framing, one dominant camera movement, lighting, color, motion intensity, duration, aspect ratio, and what must remain stable. For example: “black performance coupe, wet industrial road at night, low tracking three-quarter angle, one controlled lateral camera move, practical sodium and cyan light, readable silhouette, no text, vertical 9:16.” Do not stack whip pan, orbit, crash zoom, handheld shake, and drone movement in one prompt.

## Verification checklist

Watch frame-by-frame for identity drift, wheel geometry, hands, logos, license plates, reflections, text artifacts, impossible shadows, and motion that does not match the sound. Keep generated inserts short and place them where a viewer can accept a stylized cut. Label AI-generated or transformed media in project notes and preserve the original source.
